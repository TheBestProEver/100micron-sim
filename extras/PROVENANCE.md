# Provenance of the optional extras (extras/)

Generated from `extras/dolle_extras/provenance.py` (do not edit by hand; run `python -m dolle_extras sources --write`). The main table is ../PROVENANCE.md.

These parts of DOLL-E are optional: the simulator (dolle_sim, v0.5 engine by default) and the readable core (dolle_core) do not need them. The v0.6 engine is a second, more detailed model of the flight: every grain is its own body and every contact has sliding, rolling and twisting, where the default engine treats stuck grains as rigid clumps and holds wall grains until they are pulled off. It shares the contact law, the materials and the forces with dolle_sim.

Each component lists how it is checked: 19 automated checks of the v0.6 engine (python -m dolle_extras verify; CHECKS.md, Part 2) and the tests in extras/tests.

**Origin key:** *published*: equation or value from the cited paper · *standard*: textbook physics or numerics · *derived*: derived or adapted for DOLL-E from standard physics · *assumed*: a modelling choice or value we chose.

| **Regimes: free flight, contact dynamics, rest, rolling**<br>`engine.py: FullContactSim` | *derived*: The full model at nanosecond steps for 900 s would take ~10^4 core-days. The engine uses it wherever something can happen and exact or bounded treatments elsewhere: (1) a grain touching nothing flies on the exact free-flight integrator until the earliest time it could come within 0.1 um of anything (straight-line relative motion plus bounds on every acceleration), then joins the contact dynamics; once it touches nothing again it leaves (exact free flight, or slaved rolling on its wall) up to the earliest time it could come within 0.1 um of anything, with the same time-resolved bounds, and joins again then. (2) A grain resting on a wall, or a resting cluster anchored to a wall, is held static while a certificate shows that every contact stays inside all of its elastic limits (pull-off, sliding, rolling, twisting) with a 2% margin for the loads over the step: inside the limits nothing plastic can change, so the grain cannot move (clusters: linear statics from the contact kernel's own tangent, statics.py). It wakes at every load breakpoint, during kHz loads (releases, vibration) or when the certificate fails, and joins the contact dynamics the moment a moving grain comes within 0.2 um of it (between contact-list builds no grain in the dynamics may move further than its clearance to the nearest sleeper); on waking it is placed at the exact static equilibrium of the current load (elastic deflection included). A grain or cluster goes to sleep when its residual motion (kinetic plus elastic energy) could change no contact load by more than 1% of that contact's margin (for clusters, the exact bound over the energy ellipsoid, sqrt(2 E J K^-1 J^T)); a cluster still rocking sleeps with its residual motion kept as exact linear response (modes of M u'' + C u' + K u = 0 with the kernel's own stiffness and dashpots and the rotating-frame terms) if every mode decays, the residual moves no grain by more than a tenth of the capture distance, and every contact keeps 5x the margin with the most the residual can add; the certificate includes that bound, and on waking the residual is added back, evolved to that time. (3) A wall-anchored cluster that keeps moving (a stuck pair rolling across the floor) is integrated with the stiff normal and sliding modes of every contact held on their quasi-static values (Lagrange multipliers; slaved.py) and the rolling and twisting springs of the kernel resolved at 1/400 of their period; each slaved cluster is first run over the whole step, and the path it takes is its envelope for everyone else. (3b) A grain on one wall that rolls or rocks is integrated with its stiff normal and sliding modes held on their quasi-static values (roll1.py) at 1/200 of the rocking period, and goes back to the full model near pull-off or the sliding limit, or when it could reach anything. (4) Every step is verified after it is taken: the speed of every grain in the contact dynamics against the speed bound other grains' capture times assumed for it, every rolling grain's path against its rolling envelope, and every grain that entered the contact dynamics during the step (from the time it entered) against every grain advanced outside it at the same time. If any check fails the step is redone from its start with those grains in the full model from the start, so no interaction can be missed.<br>*numerical method written for DOLL-E; quasi-static bounds: standard single-degree-of-freedom dynamics (e.g. Chopra, Dynamics of Structures)* | Regimes against the full model: a struck row of resting grains, a rolling grain beside it and a grain landing on it, run by the engine and with every grain in the contact dynamics throughout, give the same contacts and outcomes and agree within the contact dynamics' own step error (engine_regimes; the run uses on-approach joins and step redos). Hand-off: two grains flown, captured, collided and released by the engine match the DEM alone on the engine's own time grid to round-off, and a fine DEM reference within the DEM's own grid-phase spread (engine_handoff). Rest: a grain under a slowly rising load in the full model deflects exactly by the static amount the engine applies on waking (0.05 nm of 15 nm) and rolls only after the certificate fails (sleep_exact). Cluster statics: the kernel's stiffness by central differences matches the analytic normal and tangential stiffness to 2e-8 and the static response to a 1 g load to 1e-12 (cluster_statics). Slaved rolling: matches the full model to 1.1e-5 of the rolled path under 0.7-3 kHz loads and 25 rad/s of spin (roll1_vs_full). Residual motion: a rocking pair slept with it and woken 2 ms later matches the full model to 0.07 pm and 4e-5 rad/s, against 937 pm and 0.19 rad/s with the residual dropped (linres). Each mission summary reports the number of verified steps, redone steps and on-approach joins.<br>`verify`: engine_regimes, engine_handoff, sleep_exact, cluster_statics, roll1_vs_full, linres, slaved_vs_full | Clusters that touch no wall (aggregates in flight) are always integrated in the full model. When a grain is put to sleep its residual motion (below 1% of every contact's margin) is dropped. |
