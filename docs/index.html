When does space dust stick together?

Planets start as dust grains that collide and stick. Our University of Chicago NASA RockSat experiment, DOLL-E, measures how slowly ~100 µm carbon and silicate grains must collide to stick. This simulator recreates that experiment in zero gravity, so we can predict the answer and design the payload.

How it works

Pick the dust.
Carbon, silica and other grains, any size, any number.
Run it.
Thousands of grains collide in a simulated rocket chamber. Every bump is computed from real contact physics.
Read the answer.
The speed below which grains stick, and what our cameras would actually measure.

What we found so far

39 mm/s
Graphite (carbon) grains stick below this speed
6 mm/s
Silica grains stick below this speed: carbon is ~6× stickier
2 cameras
are needed: one camera gets the answer wrong by up to 6×

Can I trust it?

Yes, and you can check it yourself: the Verify page runs 16 physics tests in your browser. The engine also agrees with the independent REBOUND N-body code, and its physics went through an adversarial review whose findings were all fixed. The one big unknown is how sticky real grain surfaces are. That is exactly what the flight will measure.
