# Provenance: where every part of DOLL-E comes from

Generated from `dolle_sim/provenance.py` (do not edit by hand; run `python tools/build_site.py`).

**None of the DOLL-E physics comes from REBOUND or LAMMPS.** No code from either is copied, translated or called by the simulator, and it runs with both unavailable (tested).

REBOUND is used in one optional file, **dolle_sim/rebound_check.py** (417 of 11292 lines). That file re-runs a simplified chamber inside REBOUND as an outside check of our trajectory integration and collision bookkeeping; it does not check the sticking physics. Two of our methods are standard ones REBOUND also uses (second-order leapfrog / velocity Verlet and direct-sum gravity), and our collision detection is related to its 'line' mode, all written independently.

LAMMPS, a widely used open-source particle code, is likewise used only in one optional file, **dolle_sim/lammps_check.py** (195 lines). That file compares the JKR contact with LAMMPS's own implementation. With the same rule for when a contact breaks, the undamped sticking speeds agree to 0.1-0.3%, the restitution to within 0.011 and the pull-off force from a wall to 0.14%. With DOLL-E's default rule (the one all simulations use) the undamped sticking speeds are 1.42x higher.

The physics comes from published contact-mechanics and aerosol papers, textbook mechanics and electrostatics, and some derivations and choices of our own. Of the 60 components listed: 17 published, 19 standard, 15 derived or adapted here, 9 assumed. Each lists how it is checked: 37 automated verification checks (python -m dolle_sim verify, also part of the test suite); the 26 model checks also run in your browser on the Verify tab, the full-contact engine checks need the desktop. CHECKS.md lists every check and comparison with its pass criterion and measured value. Some components have no automated check, and say so.

**What is not certain is the material data, and it matters a lot.** With the commonly used values, carbon (graphite) is predicted to stick below 38 mm/s and silica below 6.2 mm/s. Of that 6.2x ratio, 2.5x comes from the published surface energies, 1.7x from the published elastic constants and 1.5x from our assumed restitution values. The assumed damping also sets most of each threshold: without it graphite would stick only below 16 mm/s. Published estimates for dry silica in vacuum are up to ten times the commonly used surface energy, which would make silica about as sticky as graphite. Measuring this is what DOLL-E is for.

**Origin key:** *published*: equation or value from the cited paper · *standard*: textbook physics or numerics · *derived*: derived or adapted for DOLL-E from standard physics · *assumed*: a modelling choice or value we chose.

## Contact physics (decides stick or bounce)

| component | based on | how it is checked | assumptions and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **JKR adhesive contact**<br>`contact.py: JKRContact, _resolve_core` | *published*: Elastic-adhesive contact force F(a) = 4E\*a^3/(3R) - sqrt(8 pi w E\* a^3) and overlap delta(a) = a^2/R - sqrt(2 pi w a/E\*). The contact snaps on at delta = 0 and breaks only at delta_c < 0. This adhesion hysteresis is why slow grains stick. The sticking THRESHOLD is set by the hysteresis plus the viscoelastic damping (next item), and for the default materials the damping dominates. Without damping the thresholds would be 15.6 mm/s (graphite) and 3.8 mm/s (silica) instead of 38 and 6.2 mm/s, so the JKR hysteresis supplies only ~17% (graphite) and ~38% (silica) of the threshold impact energy. A 324, 301; Chokshi, Tielens & Hollenbach (1993) ApJ 407, 806* | With damping switched off, the integrated sticking speed equals the JKR hysteresis energy (3.825 vs 3.827 mm/s). The outcome depends only on the impact energy, to 1e-15.<br>`verify`: threshold, elastic, energy_scaling | JKR is the large-Tabor-parameter limit of continuum adhesion theory (accurate for mu above ~3-5). That is the JKR-DMT transition, where JKR overstates the adhesion hysteresis, so the silica sticking speed is probably overestimated. Grains are smooth spheres; real grains are rough. |
| **Viscoelastic damping**<br>`contact.py: _resolve_core` | *derived*: Damping force -2 sqrt(5/6) beta sqrt(2 E\* a m\*) times the approach speed. This is the Tsuji et al. | **Bounce impulse with friction**<br>`simulation.py: _impulse, _K_body` | *standard*: Rigid-body impact of two bodies, using the full 3x3 collision matrix at the contact point. | **Fragmentation thresholds**<br>`simulation.py: _fragment` | *published*: Erosion above 3 N_c E_break and shattering above 10 N_c E_break, where E_coll is the translational relative kinetic energy of the two bodies (normal plus tangential, no spin) and E_break is the aggregate's own grain-grain bond energy.
